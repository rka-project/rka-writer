# Manuscript self-check (optional)

This is a lightweight check within a Writer session, not an independent peer
review. Use one of the explicit reviewer skills when the researcher wants a
separate referee or mock-panel assessment.

Use this as an advisory whole-document read, not an automatic score or approval
gate. The researcher decides which revisions serve the paper.

## First pass: the argument

- Can a quick reader state the problem, insight, contributions, and main result?
- Does each section advance the same central story?
- Are challenges introduced before the mechanisms that address them?
- Do research questions, experiments, results, and conclusions line up?
- Are transitions driven by reasoning rather than by record order?

## Second pass: explanation and terminology

- Is every project-specific term defined before use?
- Are standard field terms preferred over invented synonyms?
- Could a non-specialist CS reader follow the system and threat model?
- Are important mechanisms explained rather than merely named?
- Does the prose use plain academic language without adjective overload?

## Third pass: evidence and scope

- Is every factual, comparative, empirical, and literature claim supported?
- Are observations distinguished from interpretations?
- Are central limitations described accurately and proportionately?
- Are figures and tables interpreted in the text, not left to speak alone?
- Has any placeholder or unsupported result been mistaken for completed work?

## Final pass: usefulness

Preserve necessary detail, especially methods and main-text results. Remove
repetition and digressions, but do not shorten the paper at the cost of logic or
reproducibility. Confirm that the conclusion answers the questions the
introduction promised.
