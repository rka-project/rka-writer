# Persuasive framing with research integrity (optional)

Use this reference when deciding how strongly to present a contribution or how
to discuss scope. It provides judgment prompts, not a disclosure form.

## Advocate the strongest supported story

State the practical or scientific problem clearly, explain the central insight,
and connect each contribution to a concrete capability or finding. Prefer
specific verbs and measurable outcomes over promotional adjectives. A reader
should understand why the work matters before encountering implementation
detail.

Distinguish what was designed, implemented, measured, observed, and inferred.
Evidence can support a bounded but meaningful claim; it need not establish an
universal claim for the contribution to be valuable.

## Handle boundaries proportionately

Describe material limitations when omitting them would make a central claim
misleading or prevent a reader from interpreting the evidence. State the
supported scope positively and concisely. Do not fill the paper with every
untested condition, hypothetical weakness, or speculative reviewer objection.

When a material boundary can be addressed through framing, narrow the claim to
what the study actually establishes and explain why that scope remains useful.
Never invent an experiment, result, citation, or deployment claim to make the
story stronger.

## Revise for a quick reader

Check whether the title, abstract, introduction, figures, and conclusion tell a
consistent story. Make the main contribution and principal evidence easy to
find. Put detail where it earns its space; do not remove necessary explanation
merely to make the draft shorter.
