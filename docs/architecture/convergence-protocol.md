# Convergence Protocol

Writer advances through three connected loops.

## Epistemic loop

The researcher and Writer select the paper question, publication claims,
evidence uses, warrants, scope, and qualifications from reviewed Core knowledge.
The loop ends when downstream discourse planning has an admitted semantic
contract, not merely when retrieval returns enough records.

## Discourse loop

The approved meaning is organized into a Paper Spine, section roles, narrative
moves, paragraph contracts, sentence intents, and transitions. The loop ends
when each planned sentence has a distinct function and the paragraph's entry,
exit, and reader question are coherent.

## Linguistic loop

Concepts are matched to field-appropriate labels, exact terms are locked, and
sentence intents are realized one at a time. Bounded cohesion edits may improve
flow without changing semantic contracts.

## Decision scheduling

The next decision is the unresolved, dependency-ready choice with the greatest
downstream consequence. Mechanical details are handled without interruption;
meaning-changing proposals are previewed. Reopening a high-level artifact
requires a reason such as new evidence, changed scope, or an explicit
researcher pivot.

## Convergence signal

The system does not equate fewer edits with convergence. It monitors stable
high-level artifacts, reopened decisions, stale dependents, accepted/rejected
alternatives, and semantic oscillation. A manuscript can be fluent while the
Authoring Graph remains unconverged.
